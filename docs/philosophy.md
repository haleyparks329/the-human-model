# Philosophy

The Human Model is grounded in a simple tension: software needs stable representations to be useful, while human lives are contextual, changing, and only partly knowable.

The answer is not to abandon structure. It is to make structure honest about provenance, time, uncertainty, authority, and correction.

## A model is an accountable simplification

The Human Model is not the person. It is a bounded domain representation built for particular decisions. It should expose what it leaves out and who can revise it.

Canonical state is useful because systems need a dependable operational view. It is dangerous when “canonical” is mistaken for permanent truth. The architecture therefore keeps history, support, and correction close to the state it serves.

## Evidence before fluency

Well-written model output can sound more certain than its support warrants. Every interpretation and claim should remain distinguishable from its evidence. Unknown, absent, conflicting, and stale are meaningful states—not inconveniences to fill with plausible language.

## Continuity without capture

Continuity means that a correction, preference, plan, and relevant outcome can matter later. It does not mean placing a complete person-shaped record into every prompt or service. Bounded projections should reveal only what a consumer needs for its role and authority.

## Adaptation with consent and judgment

Repeated corrections, abandoned workflows, and workaround behavior can reveal poor fit. The system should treat these as invitations to investigate, not proof of intent. A desire path is design evidence; it is not automatic permission to infer or act.

As impact or ambiguity rises, human review should become stronger. Automation should reduce clerical effort while keeping consequential judgment visible.

## One model, many interfaces

Chat, dashboards, spreadsheets, and specialist residents are ways to interact with the model. They are replaceable. The durable work lies in domain ownership, evidence lineage, state transitions, and review.

## Practical commitments

- Preserve the bodybuilding and performance origin as a real proving ground.
- Separate observed, inferred, and confirmed information.
- Make source conflicts and missingness explicit.
- Record hypotheses and predictions before claiming learning from outcomes.
- Keep corrections traceable.
- Prefer minimum-sufficient projections over universal context.
- Do not make [Bridget](https://github.com/haleyparks329/bridget-architecture), Coach, or any interface the owner of the person.
- Reject automatic high-impact action without appropriate authority.
- Describe current evidence precisely, especially when it comes from one-person dogfooding.

The aspiration is not a system that knows a person completely. It is a system that can remain useful while staying corrigible about what it thinks it knows.
